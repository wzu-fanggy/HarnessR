As the content of G2 cosolvent increases, the flame-retardant property of TEP-based electrolyte gradually disappears, where electrolyte with TEP:G2 vol. ratio of 6:4 endows the highest ionic conductivity and nonflammable nature (Figs. S1 and S2). Moreover, $2.0\mathrm{M}$ $\mathrm{NaClO_4}$ -TEP/G2 (6:4 vol. %) +5 wt % VC also exhibits the highest reversible capability with stable long-term cycling of PB||HC full cells in Fig. S3, and the designed electrolyte is denoted as N-TEP/G2 in the following. As shown in Fig. S4, the N-TEP/G2 electrolyte exhibits a slightly higher oxidation potential (4.80 V) than N-TEP (4.65 V) in Na||stainless steel cells, indicating its good electrochemical stability.
