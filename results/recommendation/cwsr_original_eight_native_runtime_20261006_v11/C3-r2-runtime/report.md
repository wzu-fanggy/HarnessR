- 配方原文 [0004_Chang 等 - 2025 - Solvent-Fluorine-Free Anion-Enriched and Non-Flammable Electrolyte Enables Safe Sodium-Ion Battery::sec1::child3]：Furthermore, the fluorination strategy relies on the controlled decomposition of fluorinated solvents or fluorine-rich additives to form a NaF-enriched inorganic interface, which significantly improves interfacial stability. $^{[24-26]}$ However, low-polarity solvents (e.g., cyclic ethers) used in weakly solvating strategies suffer from insufficient dissociation of sodium salts, resulting in limited ionic conductivity and flammability concerns due to their low flash points. Concentrated electrolytes and fluorination systems are also associated with high-cost concerns. Therefore, addressing the coupled challenges of temperature-resistant bulk/interface stability, cost-effectiveness, and safety remains a significant obstacle for large-scale energy storage applications. Herein, we design a flame-retardant hybrid electrolyte by combining fire-retardant triethyl phosphate (TEP) and low-freezing-point and weak-coordination methyl propionate (MP) solvents, which may offer the advantages of an anion-reinforced weak solvation structure, high nonflammability, and enhanced ionic conductivity at medium concentration (2 M). Fire-retardant TEP serves as the primary solvent to ensure salt solubility and nonflammability, while the introduction of MP, characterized by a lower freezing points and low dipole moment, aims to enhance ionic conductivity, reduce solvation energy, and facilitate the incorporation of additional anions into the Na $^{+}$ solvation structure.
- 配方原文 [0004_Chang 等 - 2025 - Solvent-Fluorine-Free Anion-Enriched and Non-Flammable Electrolyte Enables Safe Sodium-Ion Battery::sec2::child2]：The Na+-MP complex exhibits a low binding energy (-1.38 eV), consistent with its low DN value, further validating the weak coordination ability of MP. PF6− possesses the lowest highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) energy level (-10.16 eV), indicating strong resistance to electron loss and enhanced oxidative stability. Moreover, its low lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) suggests preferential decomposition at the negative electrode, thereby improving interfacial compatibility with TEP. Based on optimizations in nonflammability, ionic conductivity and long cycle performance, the 2.0 M NaPF6 in TEP/MP +1 wt% VC (6:4 by volume ratio, denoted as NTM) is selected as the optimal electrolyte for further investigation (Figures S2 and S4, Supporting Information), with 2.0 M NaPF6 dissolved in TEP +1 wt% VC (denoted as NT) and traditional carbonate electrolyte 1.0 M NaPF6 in PC (denoted as NP) serving as control groups. Moreover, the NTM electrolyte not only exhibits a reduced contact angle with the separator (Figure S5, Supporting Information), indicative of decreased electrolyte viscosity and improved wettability, but also shows an oxidation potential of up to 4.52 V and a lower reduction potential (Figure S6, Supporting Information).
