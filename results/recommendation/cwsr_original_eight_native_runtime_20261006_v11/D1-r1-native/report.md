- 配方原文 [0030_Li 等 - 2025 - An electrolyte design strategy for commercial all-climate pouch sodium-ion batteries::sec7]：3.1. Design and performance of the functional electrolyte
To begin with, in order to pre-evaluate the practicability of ether and carbonate ester as the electrolyte in pouch cells, comparative tests were first carried out, as shown in Fig. S1. Although the ether electrolytes have a high initial capacity, their cycling stability is poor, with a capacity retention rate of only 77.38 after 105 cycles, while the carbonate electrolytes have a capacity retention rate of 87.49 %. The gas production behaviour of ether and carbonate ester during long-term cycling at a cut-off voltage of 4.0 V at 45°C was monitored using differential electrochemical mass spectrometry (Fig. S2). It was observed that ether produced a greater quantity of gases, including methane and carbon monoxide, in comparison to carbonate ester. This may be a contributing factor to the unsuitability of ether for use in pouch cells, due to the rapid consumption of electrolyte, which results in a rapid decline in battery capacity. The electrolyte formula employs a combination of 1 mol/L NaPF $_{6}$ , cyclic ester EC and linear ester EMC in a mass ratio of 3:7 (called Baseline electrolyte), augmented by the incorporation of 3 wt% TMSS as a functional additive. The introduction of linear esters engenders a reduction in viscosity and an enhanced capacity to dissolve sodium salts in the electrolyte as a whole, thereby facilitating an improvement in the fluidity and ion conductivity of the electrolyte. Compounds containing sulfone groups, such as 1,3-propanesultone (PS), have been demonstrated to have excellent effects in inhibiting gas production and improving high-temperature performance (Fig. 1a). Consequently, they have been introduced into electrolytes and have become widely used. Compounds containing siloxane structures, such as trimethylsilyl isocyanate, exhibit high reactivity due to the facile rupture of their Si-O bonds. This enables effective removal of moisture and acid from the electrolyte and protection of the electrode electrolyte interface phase [25,26]. Therefore, the incorporation of TMSS is anticipated to combine the aforementioned effects with functional electrolytes, thereby achieving high-performance NFM/HC SIBs.
